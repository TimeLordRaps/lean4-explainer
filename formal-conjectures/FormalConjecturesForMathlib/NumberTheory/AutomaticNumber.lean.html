Automatic numbers

A real number is automatic in base b if its base-b digit sequence is generated by a finite automaton. Two readings of this are in use, and they differ:

  • the classical one, that the digit sequence is b-automatic;
  • the broad one, that the digit sequence is k-automatic for some k ≥ 2, which need not be b.

The broad reading is the weaker hypothesis on the number, so a theorem that rules out automatic numbers is stronger when stated with it. Adamczewski and Faverjon prove their two-base rigidity results in the broad form.

Only the digits after the radix point are used, as in NormalNumber.digitSeq. The integer part of a real number contributes finitely many digits, so it does not affect automaticity.

References:

  • AF20 Adamczewski, Boris, and Colin Faverjon. "Mahler's method in several variables and finite automata." arXiv:2012.08283 (2020). Appendix A.
  • [AS03] Allouche, Jean-Paul, and Jeffrey Shallit. "Automatic Sequences: Theory, Applications, Generalizations." Cambridge University Press, 2003.

  • AutomaticSequence.IsAutomaticInBase: the classical reading.
  • AutomaticSequence.IsBroadlyAutomaticInBase: the broad reading.
