    If $1<k<n-1$ then $\binom{n}{k}$ is divisible by a prime $p\leq n/2$ (except $\binom{7}{3}=\binom{7}{4}=5\cdot 7$).

    A conjecture of Erdős and Selfridge [ErGr80]. Proved by Ecklund [Ec69], who made the stronger conjecture that whenever $n>k^2$ the binomial coefficient $\binom{n}{k}$ is divisible by a prime $p<n/k$. Discussed in problem B31 and B33 of Guy's collection [Gu04]. Stronger forms of this conjecture are 1094 and 1095.

    Ecklund's theorem is stated for $n \geq 2k$ with the single exception $\binom{7}{3}$; by the symmetry $\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k}$ this is the form above, where $\binom{7}{4}$ is the second exception. The prime bound is $p \leq n/2$ (that is, $2p \leq n$); see erdos_384.variants.strict for the strict inequality.
