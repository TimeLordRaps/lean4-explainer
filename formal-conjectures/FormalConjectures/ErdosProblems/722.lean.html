    Let $k>r$ and $n$ be sufficiently large in terms of $k$ and $r$. Does there always exist a block $r-(n,k,1)$ design (or Steiner system with parameters $(n,k,r)$), provided the trivial necessary divisibility conditions $\binom{k-i}{r-i}\mid \binom{n-i}{r-i}$ are satisfied for every $0\leq i<r$? That is, can one find a family of $\binom{n}{r}\binom{k}{r}^{-1}$ many subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, all of size $k$, such that any $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}$ of size $r$ is contained in exactly one set in the family?

    This was proved by Keevash [Ke14] for all $(r,k)$.
