  2. theorem Erdos447.erdos_447.parts.i How large can a union-free collection $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $[n]$ be? sorry line 50 source

    How large can a union-free collection $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $[n]$ be? By union-free we mean there are no solutions to $A\cup B=C$ with distinct $A,B,C\in \mathcal{F}$. Must $\lvert \mathcal{F}\rvert =o(2^n)$?

    In [Er65b] Erdős reported that the estimate $\lvert \mathcal{F}\rvert=o(2^n)$ was proved in unpublished work by Sárközy and Szemerédi.

  3. theorem Erdos447.erdos_447.parts.ii How large can a union-free collection $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $[n]$ be? sorry line 63 source

    How large can a union-free collection $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $[n]$ be? By union-free we mean there are no solutions to $A\cup B=C$ with distinct $A,B,C\in \mathcal{F}$. Perhaps even $$\lvert \mathcal{F}\rvert <(1+o(1))\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}?$$

    Solved by Kleitman [Kl71], who proved $$\lvert \mathcal{F}\rvert <(1+o(1))\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.$$
