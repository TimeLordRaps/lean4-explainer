    What is the largest $k$ such that in any permutation of $\mathbb{Z}$ there must exist a monotone $k$-term arithmetic progression $x_1 < \cdots < x_k$?

    Here a permutation of $\mathbb{Z}$ is a one-sided arrangement $a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$ of the integers, i.e. a bijection $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}$, and a monotone $k$-term arithmetic progression is a subsequence $a_{i_1}, \ldots, a_{i_k}$ with $i_1 < \cdots < i_k$ forming an increasing or decreasing arithmetic progression.
