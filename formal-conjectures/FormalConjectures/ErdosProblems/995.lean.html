Erdős Problem 995

References:

  • erdosproblems.com/995
  • [Er49] Erdős, P., On the strong law of large numbers, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. (1949), 329-334.

Let $n_1 < n_2 < \cdots$ be a lacunary sequence of integers and $f \in L^2([0,1])$ with $\int_0^1 f = 0$. Estimate the growth of $$S_N(\alpha) = \sum_{k \le N} f(\{\alpha n_k\})$$ for almost all $\alpha$, where $\{\cdot\}$ denotes the fractional part. In particular, is it true that $S_N(\alpha) = o\!\left(N \sqrt{\log\log N}\right)$ for almost all $\alpha$?

Erdős [Er49] constructed a lacunary sequence and a mean-zero $f \in L^2([0,1])$ for which, for every $\varepsilon > 0$, $\limsup_N S_N(\alpha) / (N (\log\log N)^{1/2 - \varepsilon}) = \infty$ for almost all $\alpha$, and believed this lower bound to be close to the truth.

The mean-zero hypothesis $\int_0^1 f = 0$ is the natural normalisation: otherwise the sum has a linear main term coming from the average of $f$.
