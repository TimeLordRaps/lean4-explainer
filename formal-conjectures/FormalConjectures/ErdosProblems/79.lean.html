    We say $G$ is Ramsey size linear if $R(G,H)\ll m$ for all graphs $H$ with $m$ edges and no isolated vertices.

    Are there infinitely many graphs $G$ which are not Ramsey size linear but such that all of its proper subgraphs are?

    Asked by Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau, and Schelp [EFRS93]. $K_4$ was long the only known example. Wigderson [Wi24] proved that there are infinitely many such graphs.
