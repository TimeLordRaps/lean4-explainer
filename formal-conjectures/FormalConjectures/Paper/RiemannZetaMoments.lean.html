Moments of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line

The moments $\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T |\zeta(1/2 + it)|^{2k}\,dt$ are conjectured to be asymptotic to $c_k (\log T)^{k^2}$ for some constants $c_k$. This is a theorem for $k = 1$ (Hardy–Littlewood, $c_1 = 1$) and $k = 2$ (Ingham, $c_2 = 1/(2\pi^2)$), and these are the only proven cases. Conrey–Ghosh and Conrey–Gonek conjectured $c_3 = 42 a_3 / 9!$ and $c_4 = 24024 a_4 / 16!$, with $a_k$ the arithmetic factor $a_k = \prod_p (1 - 1/p)^{k^2} \sum_{m \ge 0} \binom{m+k-1}{m}^2 p^{-m}$; Keating–Snaith conjectured $c_k = a_k f_k$ with $f_k = \prod_{j=0}^{k-1} j!/(j+k)!$ from random matrix theory, recovering $g_k = (k^2)!\,f_k = 1, 2, 42, 24024$.

The statements and constants below are taken from §3.4.5 of [Co26].
