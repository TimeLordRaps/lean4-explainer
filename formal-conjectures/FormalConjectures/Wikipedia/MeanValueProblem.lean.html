Mean value problem

Reference:

  • Wikipedia
  • The fundamental theorem of algebra and complexity theory by Steve Smale

Given a complex polynomial $p$ of degree $d ≥ 2$ and a complex number $z$ there is a critical point $c$ of $p$, such that $|p(z)-p(c)|/|z-c| ≤ K* |p'(z)|$ for $K=1$.

The conjecture has been proven for:

  • K = 4 The fundamental theorem of algebra and complexity theory by Steve Smale
  • K = (d-1)/d at a point $z$ if the normalised polynomial $p(X + z) - p(z)$ has only real roots, or if all its nonzero roots have the same norm. Critical points and values of complex polynomials by David Tischler

    The following tighter bound depending on the degree $d$ of the polynomial $p$ has been shown by Tischler. Let $z$ be a complex number such that the normalised polynomial $p(X + z) - p(z)$ has only real roots, i.e. every $x$ with $p(x) = p(z)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Im} x = \operatorname{Im} z$. Then there is a critical point $c$ of $p$ such that $|p(z)-p(c)|/|z-c| \le (d-1)/d \cdot |p'(z)|$.
