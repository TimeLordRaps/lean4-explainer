The symbol length of $K^M_n(\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m))/p$

For a field $F$ and $\ell \ge 1$, the Milnor K-group $K^M_n(F)/\ell$ is generated by the symbols $\{a_1, \dots, a_n\}$ with $a_i \in F^\times$, and the symbol length of a class is the least number of symbols in a decomposition of the class as a sum of symbols. The symbol length of $K^M_n(F)/\ell$ is the supremum of the symbol lengths of its classes. When $\ell$ is invertible in $F$, the norm residue isomorphism theorem (the Bloch–Kato conjecture, [Voevodsky2011]) gives $K^M_n(F)/\ell \cong H^n(F, \mu_\ell^{\otimes n})$, so this is also the symbol length of Galois cohomology.

The symbol length problem asks, for a given field $F$ and integers $n$ and $\ell$, for a $k$ such that the symbol length of every class in $K^M_n(F)/\ell$ is at most $k$, for example in terms of a dimension of the field. That a class is a sum of some number of symbols is part of the presentation of $K^M_n(F)$ recalled above, so the content lies entirely in the bound. In degree $2$, over a field containing a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, Merkurjev and Suslin's theorem [MerkurjevSuslin1983] that the norm residue map is an isomorphism identifies $K^M_2(F)/\ell$ with the $\ell$-torsion of the Brauer group and symbols with symbol algebras of degree $\ell$; that is the form in which the question is usually studied, namely how many symbol algebras are needed to represent a central simple algebra of exponent $\ell$. Becher and Hoffmann [BecherHoffmann2004] named the invariant $\lambda_p$ in that degree and asked it for exactly the fields considered below (their Question 4.6). The general form stated here is Problem 2.1.3.12 of Krashen's PCMI notes; Krashen notes there that over complex function fields in at least three variables no bound is known beyond Matzri's in degree $2$ and the case of $\ell$ a power of $2$, which comes from the theory of quadratic forms, and that very little is known in degree $3$ and higher [Krashen2024, §2.1.3.4].

This file states the problem for the rational function fields $\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m)$ and a prime $\ell = p$: determine the symbol length of $K^M_n(\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m))/p$ as a function of $m$, $n$ and $p$, where the symbol length is infinite if there is no bound.

What is known:

  • in degree $0$ every symbol is the empty product $1$, so $K^M_0(F)/p = \mathbb{Z}/p$ has symbol length $p - 1$ for every field $F$;
  • in degree $1$ every class of $K^M_1(F)/p = F^\times/(F^\times)^p$ is a single symbol, so the symbol length is at most $1$;
  • $\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m)$ has cohomological dimension $m$ [Serre1997, Ch. II, §4.2], so $K^M_n(\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m))/p = 0$ for $n > m$ and the symbol length is $0$;
  • $\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m)$ is a $C_m$ field (Tsen–Lang, [Lang1952]), so by Matzri's theorem [Matzri2016, Theorem 8.2] every class in $K^M_2(\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m))/p^t$ is a sum of at most $t(p^{m-1} - 1)$ symbols when $m \ge 1$;
  • in degree $2$ a tensor product of $m - 1$ symbol algebras of degree $p$ over $\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m)$ can be a division algebra, so the symbol length is at least $m - 1$ for $m \ge 1$ [BecherHoffmann2004, Proposition 4.5], by an argument going back to Nakayama.

In degree $2$, Becher and Hoffmann ask whether that lower bound is the answer, that is whether the symbol length is exactly $m - 1$ [BecherHoffmann2004, Question 4.6]. For $m = n = 2$, the smallest case not settled above, this asks whether every class of $K^M_2(\mathbb{C}(x_1, x_2))/p$ is a single symbol; they call it "a striking open question" and note that it holds for $p \le 3$ by a theorem of Artin [BecherHoffmann2004, Introduction]. Since $m - 1$ does not involve $p$, their question is an instance of the expectation recorded in [Krashen2024, §2.1.3.4] that a bound can be taken independent of $\ell$. See also the survey [Krashen2016].

The definitions MilnorK, MilnorK.symbol, MilnorK.grade and MilnorK.symbolLengthBounds are in FormalConjecturesForMathlib/FieldTheory/MilnorKTheory.lean.

    Becher and Hoffmann's question [BecherHoffmann2004, Question 4.6]: is the symbol length of $K^M_2(\mathbb{C}(x_1, \dots, x_m))/p$ exactly $m - 1$? Their Proposition 4.5 gives the lower bound (symbol_length_problem.variants.degree_two_ge), and Matzri's theorem the much weaker upper bound $p^{m-1} - 1$ (symbol_length_problem.variants.degree_two). For $m \le 1$ the group vanishes and the answer is $0$; for $m = 2$ the question is whether every class is a single symbol, which holds for $p \le 3$ by a theorem of Artin and is open for $p \ge 5$. Since $m - 1$ does not involve $p$, an affirmative answer would settle symbol_length_problem.variants.uniform in degree $2$.
