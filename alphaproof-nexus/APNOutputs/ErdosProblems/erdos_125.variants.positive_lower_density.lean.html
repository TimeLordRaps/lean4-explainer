    — Human-written proof companion for Erdős 125

    — Burr, Erdős, Graham, and Li (1996), p. 137

    I was surprised to discover that the lower asymptotic density is zero

    — Erdős Problems 125 discussion (Giuseppe Melfi and contributors)

    Let A contain the nonnegative integers using only digits 0 and 1 in base 3, and B the analogous set in base 4. Their sumset does not have positive lower density: along suitable scales its proportion can be made arbitrarily small.

    1. Split base-3 and base-4 expansions into top and bottom digit blocks at independently chosen scales.
    2. Irrationality of log 4/log 3 supplies arbitrarily close powers 3^k and 4^m, so the number of possible remainders grows by a factor strictly below the ambient scale.
    3. The Lean proof uses the explicit contraction 11/12 rather than the companion's expository 0.99; either fixed factor below one yields geometric decay.
    4. density_tends_to_zero is converted to (A+B).lowerDensity=0, making both sides of answer(False) ↔ 0<lowerDensity false.
    5. Historical provenance: Burr, Erdős, Graham, and Li asked the density question in 1996. The current Erdős Problems record explains why the formal target adopts the lower-density interpretation.
