USA Mathematical Olympiad 2006, Problem 5

A mathematical frog jumps along the number line. The frog starts at 1, and jumps according to the following rule: if the frog is at integer n, then it can jump either to n + 1 or to n + 2 ^ (mₙ + 1), where 2 ^ mₙ is the largest power of 2 that is a factor of n. Show that if k ≥ 2 is a positive integer and i is a nonnegative integer, then the minimum number of jumps needed to reach 2 ^ i * k is greater than the minimum number of jumps needed to reach 2 ^ i.
