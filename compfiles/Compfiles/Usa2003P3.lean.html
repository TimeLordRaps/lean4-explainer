USA Mathematical Olympiad 2003, Problem 3

Let n be a positive integer. For every sequence of integers A = (a₀, a₁, a₂, . . . , aₙ) satisfying 0 ≤ aᵢ ≤ i, for i = 0, . . . , n, we define another sequence t(A) = (t(a₀), t(a₁), t(a₂), . . . , t(aₙ)) by setting t(aᵢ) to be the number of terms in the sequence A that precede the term aᵢ and are different from aᵢ. Show that, starting from any sequence A as above, fewer than n applications of the transformation t lead to a sequence B such that t(B) = B.
