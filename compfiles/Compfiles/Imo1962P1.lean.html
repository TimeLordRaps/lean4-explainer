International Mathematical Olympiad 1962, Problem 1

Find the smallest natural number $n$ which has the following properties:

(a) Its decimal representation has 6 as the last digit.

(b) If the last digit 6 is erased and placed in front of the remaining digits, the resulting number is four times as large as the original number $n$.

  15. problem Imo1962P1.imo1962_p1 IMO 1962 Problem 1: moving the last digit 6 to the front line 140 source

    L4DEL explanation

    The smallest natural number ending in 6 whose value becomes four times larger when that 6 is moved to the front is 153846.

    1. Problem: find the smallest natural n whose last digit is 6 and such that moving that 6 to the front gives 4n. The statement is IsLeast {n | ProblemPredicate n} solution, where the answer solution is declared as 153846.
    2. Solution (complete, no sorry): write n = 10c + 6 so the condition becomes 6 * 10^(number of digits of c) + c = 4n.
    3. Cases on the digit count of c: zero to four digits are impossible, five digits forces c = 15384, and six or more digits give nothing smaller. A separate lemma checks that 153846 satisfies the condition.

    See also: Imo1960P1.imo1960_p1
