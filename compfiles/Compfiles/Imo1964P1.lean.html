International Mathematical Olympiad 1964, Problem 1

(a) Find all natural numbers n for which 2ⁿ - 1 is divisible by 7. (b) Prove that there is no positive integer n for which 2ⁿ + 1 is divisible by 7.

    Annotation provenance. AI-generated and not human-reviewed. Treat this explanatory draft as unverified and not mathematical authority.

    2^n is congruent to 1 modulo 7 exactly when n is a multiple of 3.

    1. Problem part (a): find all natural n with 7 dividing 2^n - 1. The formal answer is the determined set solution_set = {n | n % 3 = 0}, and the statement is n in solution_set iff 2^n is congruent to 1 mod 7.
    2. Solution (complete, no sorry): if n = 3m then 2^n = 8^m, which is 1 mod 7.
    3. Conversely write n = 3q + r; since 2^3 = 1 mod 7 the power reduces to 2^r, and r = 1 or r = 2 gives 2 or 4, not 1.
    4. Part (b), that 7 never divides 2^n + 1, is the separate declaration imo1964_p1b in the same file, proved by the same residue argument.
