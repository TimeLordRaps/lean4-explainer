International Mathematical Olympiad 1976, Problem 3

A rectangular box can be completely filled with unit cubes. If one places as many cubes as possible, each with volume 2, in the box, with their edges parallel to the edges of the box, one can fill exactly 40% of the box. Determine the possible dimensions of the box.

Formalization

The box has integer dimensions a ≤ b ≤ c. A cube of volume 2 has side length k = ∛2 (the real cube root of two). A placement of such cubes in the box, with edges parallel to the edges of the box, is formalized as a finite set P of corner positions satisfying IsPacking a b c P: every cube lies inside the box and distinct cubes have disjoint interiors (NonOverlapping).

The maximal number of cubes that fit is maxNumCubes a b c = ⌊a / k⌋₊ * ⌊b / k⌋₊ * ⌊c / k⌋₊: along an edge of integer length n exactly ⌊n / k⌋₊ cubes fit. The maximality is proved formally rather than asserted: packing_card_le shows that no packing has more cubes (the map sending a cube at (x, y, z) to the triple (⌊x / k⌋₊, ⌊y / k⌋₊, ⌊z / k⌋₊) is injective on a packing), and exists_packing shows that the grid arrangement attains the bound.

The "exactly 40%" condition is stated explicitly as 2 * P.card = 40 / 100 * (a * b * c) for a maximal packing P: the total volume of the cubes is 40% of the box volume. Since a maximal packing has P.card = maxNumCubes a b c, this is equivalent to a * b * c = 5 * (⌊a / k⌋₊ * ⌊b / k⌋₊ * ⌊c / k⌋₊), which is the form used by the integer-arithmetic core of the proof.
