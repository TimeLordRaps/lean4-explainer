  5. theorem OAI.Rokhlin.mixing_all_finite_orders Challenge statement: Ordinary mixing implies mixing of every finite order sorry line 30

    L4DEL explanation

    Every invertible mixing probability-preserving transformation is mixing of every finite order

    — OpenAI Rokhlin multiple-mixing manuscript (abstract)

    Rohlin asked in 1949 whether 2-fold mixing implies 3-fold mixing for a stationary process indexed by Z

    — Thierry de la Rue (2006), arXiv v1 abstract

    Every invertible probability-preserving transformation that is mixing for pairs of measurable sets is mixing for every finite number of sets when all successive time gaps diverge.

    1. IsMixing says μ(A ∩ T⁻ⁿB) tends to μ(A)μ(B) as the absolute integer time tends to infinity, for every measurable pair A,B.
    2. layoutTime builds ordered observation times from positive gaps. MixingOfOrder k asks the measure of the intersection of all k translated sets to converge to the product of their measures as the entire gap vector tends to infinity.
    3. The theorem assumes an arbitrary probability measure, an invertible measurable equivalence preserving that measure, and ordinary mixing. It concludes MixingOfOrder k for every k≥3; no standardness or countable-generation hypothesis appears in the Lean statement.
    4. De la Rue's 2006 paper records the twofold-to-threefold question as still open at that time. The comparator body here is sorry, paired with OAI.Dynamics.MultipleMixing.Main; the linked manuscript supplies the claimed affirmative proof exposition.

    Sources and attribution: Pinned Lean source (fd4aeeb2ee4f), OpenAI Rokhlin multiple-mixing manuscript (abstract), Pinned OpenAI formalization note 145, Thierry de la Rue (2006), arXiv v1 abstract
