    The formalization determines the sharp logarithmic exponent of the off-diagonal Ramsey number $r(5,t)$. It proves $r(5,t)=t^4/(\log t)^{3+o(1)}$ as $t\to\infty$. For every $\varepsilon>0$, the lower bound $t^4/(\log t)^{3+\varepsilon}$ holds eventually, while the upper bound is $Ct^4/(\log t)^3$ for an absolute $C>0$. The corresponding logarithmic exponent converges to three along all natural values of $t$. The formalization determines the sharp logarithmic exponent of the off-diagonal Ramsey number for every fixed integer $s\ge6$. It proves $r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}$ as $t\to\infty$. More explicitly, for each $\varepsilon>0$ the lower bound $t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+\varepsilon}$ holds eventually, while the upper bound is $C_s t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2}$. The logarithmic exponent limit is taken along all natural values of $t$.
