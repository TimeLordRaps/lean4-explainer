  15. theorem OAI.ExactModuli.exact_main Challenge statement: Reflexive midpoint convexity and diamond distortion sorry line 72
    Annotation provenance. This overlay contains 9 curated AI-written annotations and 1307 mechanical source-derived annotations. They have not been human-reviewed as explanations; treat them as unverified and not mathematical authority. Retained generation history: The 71 original curated annotations were produced in a Claude Sonnet 5.5 session at medium effort; Claude Opus 5.5 is recorded in that session as the advisor model, not the direct author.     The paper constructs a Banach space whose midpoint geometry is asymptotically uniformly convex even though no equivalent norm is asymptotically uniformly convex in the usual one-sided sense. The formalization proves that the full dual of the specified segment-norm completion is infinite-dimensional, separable, and reflexive; its averaged midpoint modulus is at least $\sqrt{1+t^2/12}-1$ for every $t>0$, while every equivalent norm fails asymptotic uniform convexity. It also proves that an embedding of a depth-$k$ countably branching diamond has distortion at least $\sqrt{1+k/12}$. The general-forest and word-forest results use the closed span of the coordinate functionals. Their conclusions retain the finite-ancestor and equivalent-norm hypotheses, including the scale $\alpha/(2\beta)$ when $0<\alpha\le\beta$ and the new norm lies between $\alpha$ and $\beta$ times the original norm; they do not identify that span with the full dual or assert reflexivity for every forest. The paper controls midpoint lenses in Banach spaces defined by tree-segment norms. The formalization proves the following estimate in both the full dual of the finite-height forest space and the coordinate predual of the infinite-tree space. If $x$ is supported on a finite ancestral set $H$, $R\ge0$, and $\|x+y\|,\|x-y\|\le R$, then the part of $y$ outside $H$ has norm at most $2\sqrt{R^2-\|x\|^2}$. The formalization also covers selected consequences for averaged midpoint moduli and separated families of vectors, together with reflexivity and renorming obstructions. This scope concerns the segment-space results; the companion results on diamond energies remain separate. The paper studies embeddings of countably branching diamond graphs into Banach spaces built from tree segments.
