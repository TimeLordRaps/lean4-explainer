  9. theorem OAI.Balanced.main_result Challenge statement: Counterexamples to Ryser’s covering conjecture sorry line 39
    Annotation provenance. AI-generated and not human-reviewed. Treat this explanatory draft as unverified and not mathematical authority.

    Ryser's covering conjecture predicts that an intersecting $r$-partite hypergraph has a vertex cover of size at most $r-1$. The formalization proves that every sufficiently large prime $q$ has a finite intersecting $(q+1)$-partite, $(q+1)$-uniform hypergraph with covering number $q+1$ and exactly $q+1$ nonisolated vertices in each part. Thus the conjecture fails even with equal part sizes. The prime threshold is existential. Ryser's covering conjecture predicts $\tau\le(r-1)\nu$ for an $r$-partite hypergraph, where $\tau$ and $\nu$ are its covering and matching numbers. The formalized constructions give finite intersecting $r$-partite $r$-uniform hypergraphs with $\nu=1$ and $\tau=r$, contradicting the bound. The ranks have the form $r=p^n+1$: one fixed prime $p\equiv2\pmod3$ works for all sufficiently large prime degrees $n$, and a second result covers every sufficiently large such prime $p$ and every sufficiently large odd $n$, with the degree threshold allowed to depend on $p$. Infinitely many ranks occur. Equal part sizes and numerical thresholds are not asserted.
