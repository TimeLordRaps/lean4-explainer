  6. theorem OAI.Barnette.main Challenge statement: Barnette's Hamiltonian-cycle conjecture sorry line 48

    L4DEL explanation

    We prove Barnette’s conjecture: every finite simple cubic bipartite planar 3-vertex-connected graph has a Hamiltonian cycle.

    — OpenAI Barnette manuscript (abstract)

    Every cubic, bipartite, polyhedral graph contains a Hamiltonian cycle.

    — Lean Arts, Meike Hopman, and Veerle Timmermans, Barnette's Conjecture (2013)

    Every finite simple planar graph that is cubic, bipartite, and remains connected after deletion of any two vertices has one cycle visiting every vertex exactly once.

    1. The statement quantifies over every finite vertex type and simple graph. Its four hypotheses are degree-three regularity, bipartiteness, a crossing-free topological plane embedding, and ThreeVertexConnected.
    2. ThreeVertexConnected also requires at least four vertices. HasHamiltonianCycle asks for a closed walk satisfying Mathlib's IsHamiltonianCycle; it is a single spanning cycle, not a disconnected spanning 2-factor.
    3. David Barnette posed the conjecture in 1969 in the language of cubic bipartite polyhedral graphs. By Steinitz's theorem, polyhedral corresponds here to planar and 3-vertex-connected.
    4. The pinned manuscript's page-one strategy passes to the spherical dual triangulation and constructs an acyclic dual edge set whose complement becomes one connected spanning cycle; the Lean statement exposes only the theorem, not that prose proof outline.
    5. Trust boundary: this comparator declaration ends in by sorry. The pinned comparator configuration pairs it with OAI.Combinatorics.Hamiltonian.Main; the site's Lean trust result, not this annotation, determines whether that separate solution module checks.
