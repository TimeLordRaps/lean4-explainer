  1. def OAI.SeymourSecondNeighborhood.Counterexample Every vertex has a strict first-neighborhood surplus line 10

    L4DEL explanation

    |N1+ (v)| > |N2+ (v)| for every v ∈ V (D).

    — OpenAI Seymour second-neighborhood manuscript

    Counterexample r says that every vertex fails GoodVertex: its exact second out-neighborhood is strictly smaller than its first. This pointwise strict inequality is the negation normal form used by the minimal-counterexample argument.

    1. Because the graph is finite, negating |N₁⁺| ≤ |N₂⁺| becomes |N₂⁺| < |N₁⁺|.
    2. The definition alone does not assert orientation or nonemptiness; those hypotheses are carried separately in the reduction theorems.

  6. def OAI.SeymourSecondNeighborhood.PositiveIndegree Every vertex has an incoming neighbor line 29

    L4DEL explanation

    Every vertex of G has positive indegree.

    — OpenAI Seymour second-neighborhood manuscript

    PositiveIndegree r requires an incoming witness u → v for each vertex v. In the reduction this property follows from strong connectivity of a minimal counterexample and survives the transitive-tournament blowup.

    1. The property is existential at each vertex rather than a numerical degree bound.
    2. Later it ensures the image of the full vertex set is again the full set and prevents degenerate rows or columns in the extremal construction.

    L4DEL explanation

    |U | + |FG2 (U )| < 2|FG (U )|

    — OpenAI Seymour second-neighborhood manuscript

    StrictSubsetGrowth r is the amplified inequality |S|+|F²(S)| < 2|F(S)| for every nonempty proper finite subset. It compares ordinary relation images, which may overlap S, rather than exact-distance neighborhoods.

    1. The left side combines the starting set with its twice-iterated image.
    2. The strict factor-two inequality is the obstruction later ruled out by the extremal pruning argument.
